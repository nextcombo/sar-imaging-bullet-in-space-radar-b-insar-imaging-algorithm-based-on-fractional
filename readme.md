# [SAR Imaging] Bullet In-Space Radar (B-InSAR) Imaging Algorithm Based on Fractional Fourier Transform [with MATLAB Code]

This paper proposes a ballistic SAR imaging algorithm based on the Fractional Fourier Transform (FrFT) for addressing the issues of large Doppler parameters variation with range and low resolution in traditional pulse compression imaging algorithms. The study first establishes a model for the echo signals from the ballistic SAR's final guidance phase, then measures the modulation frequency of the echo signals through local optimal processing, and calculates the optimal order of the FrFT accordingly. Under this optimal order, the echo signals are processed in both range and azimuth directions using the FrFT, resulting in an SAR image of the imaging area. Finally, comparative simulations and experiments using traditional pulse compression imaging algorithms and the proposed FrFT-based imaging algorithm were conducted. The experimental results show that this algorithm can achieve precise imaging of the target area; due to the independent local optimal processing of each range and azimuth echo signal during the imaging process, this method is more suitable for ballistic SAR's nonlinear flight trajectory, significantly enhancing the imaging performance of ballistic SAR. This research has significant application value in target detection and recognition, as well as in precision guidance.
Introduction
Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) offers advantages over optical and infrared sensors in terms of all-weather and all-time capabilities. As a microwave imaging radar, SAR utilizes the movement of the radar platform to achieve a large aperture antenna, thereby obtaining high-resolution radar images in azimuth direction. Applying SAR in missile guidance allows it to detect concealed and disguised targets under conditions such as darkness, fog, smoke, strong light interference, and harsh weather, making it more adaptable to the complex working environments of missiles in actual combat.
In the final guidance phase of missiles, due to the significant flight attitude changes and complex flight trajectory during the dive-down motion, as well as the large nonlinear distance walk and distortion in the echo signal, conventional SAR imaging algorithms struggle to achieve precise target focusing. Literature [5, 6] approximates the missile flight model to a straight line, thereby converting it into oblique imaging. This algorithm is applicable when the missile's horizontal velocity is relatively high, but its approximation becomes difficult when the vertical velocity is significant. Literature [7] employs subaperture processing to explore the characteristics of the missile-borne SAR signal during descent and the feasibility of imaging. This algorithm overcomes the issue of large Doppler parameter variations, achieving focused subaperture images. However, due to not utilizing all the echo signals, the azimuth resolution is reduced. Literature [8] studies the distance shift correction and phase compensation factors under conditions of constant speed dive, constant acceleration dive, and high-speed dive using the extended chirp scaling (ECS) algorithm. It proposes an equivalent orthogonal side-looking (OSL) working mode, where the target distance shift at the scene center is minimal, enabling imaging functionality. However, due to practical constraints, this mode is generally deviated from, thus limiting the use of this algorithm. Literature [9] uses an improved chirp scaling (CS) imaging algorithm for imaging processing, considering the effects of variable acceleration and employing spectral selection techniques to address the non-uniform sampling problem in the azimuth direction. However, in distance shift correction, interpolation is used, which increases computational load and the accuracy of the correction depends on the precision of the interpolation. The SAR echo signal in the azimuth direction can be approximately treated as a linear frequency modulated (LFM) signal, with typical radar transmission signals also being LFM signals. Therefore, both the range and azimuth directions of the SAR echo are LFM signals. FrFT, a linear transformation, does not introduce cross terms interference [10−12]. Amein, Clemente et al. [13−15] proposed a CS algorithm based on FrFT, replacing the FFT in traditional CS algorithm matching filter processing with FrFT, achieving high-resolution SAR imaging. However, this algorithm only theoretically analyzes the positive side-looking SAR and is not suitable for the nonlinear flight trajectory of the missile-borne SAR platform.
This paper proposes a payload SAR imaging algorithm based on FrFT, which optimizes the transform order to provide optimal angles for FrFT, enabling the given LFM signal to achieve optimal response. When the coordinate axes are rotated to match the modulation frequency, this response will reach its maximum value, thereby completing the imaging of the target. Simulation and experimental results show that this algorithm can accurately image the target area. This is because during the imaging processing, by independently localized optimal processing of each range and azimuth echo signals, the algorithm is more suitable for payload SAR platforms with nonlinear flight trajectories, significantly improving the imaging performance of payload SAR. The research findings have important application values in target detection and recognition, as well as in precise guidance.

Conclusion
This paper systematically studies the payload SAR echo signal model and its imaging technology, and proposes an imaging algorithm suitable for payload SAR platforms with nonlinear flight trajectories. Through simulation and real-measurement data, this algorithm has been validated, and compared with traditional pulse compression imaging algorithms. The results indicate that, due to the local optimal processing of each distance cell in the imaging process, corresponding transformation parameters are obtained for each distance cell, thus achieving the imaging of the target. The imaging focusing performance and quality of the algorithm proposed in this paper have been improved to some extent, making it more adaptable to payload SAR motion platforms. The research findings have certain application values in target detection and precise guidance.
